# Manual Review Cases

## Objective

Customer segmentation provides a useful overview of customer behavior, but not every customer fits perfectly into a predefined segment.

Some customers show mixed signals. These cases require human judgment before deciding on retention actions.

The following customers were selected because their behavior makes the retention decision less obvious.

---

# Case 1

Customer ID: C01562

Segment: At-Risk

Why This Case Is Interesting

The customer has purchased multiple times and generated reasonable revenue but has become inactive recently.

Decision

Send a personalized re-engagement campaign.

Reason

The customer still shows signs of past engagement and may be recoverable.

---

# Case 2

Customer ID: C03745

Why This Case Is Interesting

Decision

Reason

Customer may simply be an infrequent buyer rather than a true churn risk.

# Case 10

Customer ID: C04084

Segment: Loyal Customers

Why This Case Is Interesting

Very valuable customer but showing a slight decline in recent engagement.

Reason

---

# Key Takeaways

1. Not all customers within the same segment behave identically.
2. High-value customers deserve individual review before retention decisions.
3. Support issues frequently require human intervention.
4. Customer lifetime value should influence retention prioritization.
5. Automated segmentation should support, not replace, business judgment.

---

# Conclusion

Customer segmentation provides a strong framework for retention planning, but certain customers require manual review because their behavior does not fit a straightforward retention strategy.

Combining analytics with human judgment ensures more effective and business-focused customer retention decisions.
